**Research aims**

_Experiment 1_ investigates whether visual and tactile (vibration) motion cues enhance the categorisation of unfamiliar 3D object shapes and the generalisation of learned categories to novel exemplars.

1. Experiment 1a: Cue conditions are presented in blocked format at test.
2. Experiment 1b: Cue conditions are presented in interleaved format at test.

_Experiment 2_ investigates whether visual and tactile (vibration) motion cues enhance the categorisation of unfamiliar 3D object shapes and the generalisation of learned categories to novel exemplars, when one cue is less informative of category membership.

1. Experiment 2a: Cue conditions are presented in blocked format at test.
2. Experiment 2b: Cue conditions are presented in interleaved format at test.

_Experiment 3_ is a follow up experiment, to assess and isolate the effect of shape alone. Experiment 3 followed the same learning procedure but presented only static, non-moving objects without motion or tactile cues. This allowed us to assess the relative contribution of multisensory motion cues beyond shape.

**Research aims**

To investigate whether both motion and tactile (vibration) cues enhance the categorisation of unfamiliar 3D object shapes and generalisation to novel exemplars. 

**Method and Procedure**

The study was developed in PsychoPy (version 2024; Peirce et al., 2019) and conducted online via Pavlovia, using Android smartphones.

_Instructions_: please examine the object presented and decide to which category it belongs: either to Category A or Category B.

The total duration of the experiment was around 18 minutes.

**Stimuli**

The experiment presents with static and moving 3D objects shapes of unfamiliar objects.

1. _3D Object Shapes_: Unfamiliar objects generated by rotating 2D shapes (from the circular shape space by Li et al., 2020) 360° around the vertical axis though the Spin Function in Edit mode (degrees 360, steps 100) in Blender 3.5.0 (2023; www.blender.org)
2. _Animation_: Four motion types were implemented (swinging, jumping, rotation, shaking). Each motion lasted 2 seconds.
3. _Tactile stimuli_: Vibration patterns temporally correlated with motion patterns, composed of alternating bursts and pauses.

**Design**

A within-subjects design with four cue conditions: Shape only (static; Sv); Shape + visual motion (SvMv); Shape + tactile motion (SvMt) and Shape + visual motion + tactile motion (SvMvt).

The experiment consisted of:

1. _Supervised learning task_: Participants categorised stimuli defined by three cues (shape, motion, vibration). Feedback was given until participants reached 75% accuracy, up to 4 repetitions of the stimulus set.
   
   1.a _In Experiment 1_: all cues were 100%, fully informative.
   
   1.b _In Experiment 2_: shape was not be fully reliable for category membership (78% reliability), whereas visual and tactile motion cues are fully (100%) informative.
   
   1.c _In Experiment 3_: participants categorize stimuli defined by object shape (i.e., static, non-moving objects without motion or tactile cues).
   
2. _Categorisation test task_: Participants categorised both old and novel exemplars under the four cue conditions (Sv, SvMv, SvMt,SvMvt). For Experiment 1a, Cue conditions were blocked; while for Experiment 1b they were interleaved.

**Dependent variables**

1. Categorisation accuracy.
2. Categorisation slope at category boundary.
3. Reaction times (RTs).

**Independent variables**

_Cue Condition:_ Sv, SvMv, SvMt, SvMvt.

_**Exemplar Type:**_

1. _Learned exemplars_: Object Shapes (i.e., stimuli n. 5-13, 24 – 32 in the 3D stimuli space) presented during supervised learning;
   1.a _In Experiment 2: Learned exemplars (Informative):_ Object Shapes (i.e., stimuli n. 5-13, 24 – 32 in the 3D stimuli space) presented during supervised learning.These shapes are 100% informative of category membership. _Learned exemplars (Uninformative):_ Object Shapes (i.e., stimuli n. 3,7,22,26 in the 3D stimuli space) previously presented in the supervised learning task. These shapes are uninformative of category membership;
   1.b _In Experiment 3_ : Learned exemplars: Object Shapes (i.e., stimuli n. 5-13, 24 – 32 in the 3D stimuli space) presented during supervised learning;
2. _Novel exemplars_: Object Shapes (i.e., stimuli defined as 14 - 23 in the 3D stimuli space) not seen during training but within the same shape space.

**Covariates**

Age, sex.

**Sample size**

_Experiment 1_  : The sample size is 45 participants for Experiment 1a and of 32 participants for Experiment 1b.

_Experiment 2_ : The sample size is 33 participants for Experiment 2a and of 33 participants for Experiment 2b.

_Experiment 3_ : The sample size is 30 participants for Experiment 3.
 
Power analysis was conducted on PANGEA (https://jakewestfall.shinyapps.io/pangea/) for a within-subjects design. For the 2x4 interaction effect (Cue Condition x Exemplar), 80% power was achieved with 32 participants, assuming an effect size (Cohen’s d) of 0.3.
